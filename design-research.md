# Zenvi Frontend Redesign — Design Research

Teardowns of six reference sites the user wants to draw from, plus cross-site patterns and concrete takeaways for the Zenvi rebuild.

Sources: flora.ai, morphic.com, aliclifton.com, mosaic.so, usecardboard.com, martini.film

---

## Cross-site patterns (what they all do)

Every one of these sites — across very different product categories — leans on the same six moves. Worth treating as table stakes for the Zenvi rebuild.

1. **Hero leads with identity or capability, not features.** Nobody says "the best AI video tool." They say things like *"Your creative environment"* (Flora), *"Your Film Set for AI Videos"* (Martini), *"Edit videos at the speed of thought"* (Cardboard). The visitor sees what they'll *become*, not what the product *does*.
2. **Show, don't tell — product UI is the hero visual.** Morphic, Cardboard, Mosaic, Martini all front-load real interface screenshots or in-product video. No abstract gradients, no stock photography of people pointing at laptops.
3. **Workflow > feature list.** Flora's "Ideate → Iterate → Scale," Morphic's "Canvas / Copilot / Compose," Cardboard's "first cut to final cut" — they collapse 30 features into 3 named acts. Easier to remember, harder to forget.
4. **CTAs gate-keep, but lightly.** Hero CTA always free or low-commitment ("Get started for free," "Try it," "Open Canvas"). The enterprise/demo CTA only appears once value is established. Martini is the exception — "Request Access" only — because they're positioning exclusivity.
5. **Trust signals are visual proof, not testimonials.** Logos of recognizable customers (Pentagram, Nike, PostHog, NewsUK), named creators with linked work, press mentions from credible outlets. Star ratings and "★★★★★ life-changing!" testimonials are entirely absent.
6. **Dark, breathing, generous spacing.** Five of six sites use near-black backgrounds with high-contrast product imagery. Whitespace is the loudest design element. Typography is large, confident, often near-monospace or strong sans-serif.

## 1. Flora.ai — "Aspiration wrapped in efficiency"

**Hook:** *"Your creative environment"* → *"Bring your ideas to life faster than ever before"* → *"Every creative AI tool, one unified process"*

Three-line cascade: identity → benefit → mechanism. Avoids leading with tech. Emotional register: **liberation from tab-switching hell**.

**Arc:** Hero → meet FAUNA (personified AI co-creator) → 01 Ideate / 02 Iterate / 03 Scale → Pentagram/Lionsgate logos → 50+ model list (deferred) → case studies w/ named designers → press strip → closing line: *"A new medium needs a new canvas."*

**Tone:** Confident, not condescending. Speaks in creative vocabulary, not AI jargon. *"Never let a good idea go unexplored."* *"Thinks with you, builds with you."* Short + long sentence rhythm creates momentum.

**Resonance moves:**
- **Personification** (FAUNA isn't "the AI" — she's a co-creator who *thinks with you*). Reduces replacement anxiety.
- **Three-act workflow** as a psychological climb (idea → team → production).
- **Designer-centric framing** — Catherine Chung, Cory Dobbin, real names attached to real work. AI is the medium, not the maker.
- **Philosophical closer** — "A new medium needs a new canvas" positions Flora as a paradigm, not a tool.

## 2. Morphic.com — "Show, don't tell"

**Hook:** Hero is a large Copilot UI screenshot. Headline: *"Smart storytelling / For every creative."* Subtext: *"generate, animate, and edit effortlessly using breakthrough technologies."*

**Arc:** Three pillars (Canvas/Copilot/Compose) → editing capabilities → 25+ concrete workflow templates (time-freeze, AI headshot, virtual try-on) → technical capabilities → "Built for teams" → industry use cases → "Originals" (in-house films like *Moonlight Veil*) → closing CTA with a single still chair photo.

**Tone:** Conversational but confident. Short headlines ("Models." "3D motion.") alternate with explainers. Inclusive without condescension — *"For every creative"* but never *"for anyone."*

**Visual language:** Near-black backgrounds, bright UI screenshots, all-caps section titles ("WORKFLOWS BUILT FOR SPEED"), generous breathing between workflow cards. No fake animation on the hero — motion is shown *through* the workflows.

**Resonance moves:**
- **Workflow-as-narrative** — each template is framed as an *outcome*, not a feature. "Restyle any video in a completely new visual style. Every frame transforms, all motion stays intact." You're buying permission to make a specific vision.
- **Repetition of "Built"** — Built for Speed, Built for Teams, Built to inspire. Constructive, reliable, intentional.
- **Closing pattern break** — after 25+ vivid examples, a single still chair photo paired with "no downloads, no installs." The tool disappears; *your* story remains.

## 3. AliClifton.com — "Accessibility masquerading as sophistication"

**Hook:** *"hI! I'M Ali. Director, Writer, Creative."* Lowercase + sentence-case mash breaks the formal-portfolio fourth wall. Halo wordmark, gif-subtle motion.

**Arc:** Casual greeting → work carousel (8 thumbnails) → 12 project grid (ads, music, docs) → client logo gauntlet (Reebok, Nike, Google, EA, PlayStation, Pepsi, Red Bull, Lollapalooza, Specialized…) → personal photo + direct email + phone number.

**Tone:** *"Dreaming up crazy ideas is my sweet-spot."* *"On set is my happy place."* *"Reach out, say hi!"* Conversational, direct, self-assured without arrogance. Zero jargon.

**Resonance moves:**
- **Phone number in the footer** — most portfolios hide contact behind a form. Vulnerability builds trust faster than polish.
- **Work-first ordering** — gallery before bio. No "my journey" section. Respects the visitor's time.
- **Logo gauntlet replaces testimonials** — eleven recognizable brands. The work speaks; no testimonial quotes needed.

This is the only site in the set that's a *personal* brand, but its lessons (lowercase intimacy, phone-in-footer vulnerability, work-before-bio) translate directly to any small/founder-led product site.

## 4. Mosaic.so — "Insider language, customer-voice proof"

**Hook (current):** *"Introducing Motion — A video agent for tasteful motion design."* (They've just launched a sub-product, so it's taking the hero slot.) The parent positioning underneath: *"Mosaic — The AI Video Editing Company."*

**Arc:** Motion launch → product family (Canvas, API & Agent Skills, Motion) → "VIDEO EDITING WORKFLOWS & AUTOMATIONS" / "PROGRAMMATIC VIDEO EDITING" → 1,000+ customer line → long testimonial wall → press mentions → enterprise/audience nav (Founders, Creators, Podcasters, Marketers, Agencies).

**CTAs:** *Get Started* / *Get Early Access* / *Open Canvas* / *Explore API* / *Book a Demo* — four tiers of intent on the same page, calibrated to different visitor stages.

**Tone:** Industry-insider, not hype-y. "Tasteful motion design," "programmatic video editing," "agentic." Customer quotes do most of the emotional work:
- *"Mosaic has not just treated us like a client, but more like a partner. As a result our vertical video output on our pilot station, talkSPORT, has tripled."* — Director of Digital, NewsUK
- *"When I found Mosaic, it was a game changer… for the first time ever I was able to post 30+ times in a month."* — Co-Founder of 24/7 Artists
- *"Mosaic was voted the best product in our YC group during the Winter '25 batch."* — Managing Partner at Y Combinator

**Resonance moves:**
- **Quote-led storytelling** — letting customers describe outcomes (tripled output, 30+ posts/month, 10x'd workflow) rather than claiming them. Numbers in their voice land harder than numbers in yours.
- **Multiple CTA tiers** on one page — Free, Early Access, Demo, API docs. Different visitors need different doors.
- **Audience-segmented nav** (Founders / Creators / Podcasters / Marketers / Agencies) — each segment self-selects into the right narrative.

## 5. Cardboard (usecardboard.com) — "Contrast-reduction storytelling"

**Hook:** *"Edit videos at the speed of thought."* Sub: *"Go from raw footage to a clean, publish-ready edit in minutes."* YC badge under the fold for instant credibility.

**Arc:** Speed promise → *"First cut to final cut. No grind"* → feature showcase by use case (talking heads, vlogs, montages, podcasts) → *"An editor with superpowers"* (the AI explainer) → collaboration & search → pricing.

**Tone:** *"No grind."* *"Superpowers."* *"10x faster."* *"Describe the change."* Direct, conversational, action-verb-heavy. Speaks to creators tired of repetitive work, then quietly reassures that artistry is preserved.

**Resonance moves:**
- **Contrast-reduction framing** — current editing = slow/tedious. Cardboard = thought-speed/no grind. The whole site is a single before/after.
- **Real product UI in the hero** — timeline, waveforms, percentage indicators. Language of real editors, not concept art.
- **The "Director" agent labeled "Claude Sonnet 4.6"** — humanizes AI as a *colleague*, not a black box. Specific model name signals technical confidence.
- **Two logo strips** — ambient trust-building through repetition without aggression.

## 6. Martini.film — "Reclaiming filmmaker agency"

**Hook:** *"Your Film Set for AI Videos"* — borrows filmmaking vocabulary entirely. Subhead: *"AI Video Production for Professionals."*

**Arc:** "I want to direct, not just prompt" → three pillars (camera control, integrated pipeline, team collab) → 200+ films, named creators with portfolio links → enablement.

**Tone:** Industry-insider. *"Director's chair."* *"Pipeline."* *"XML export."* *"Most AI tools are built around prompts and seeds — not the way filmmakers think."* No superlatives. Audience explicitly named: *"creatives, studios, and agencies."*

**CTAs:** Only *"Request Access."* Gated. No "Try Free." Signals exclusivity and seriousness. The opposite tactic from Flora/Morphic.

**Resonance moves:**
- **Reclaim agency through framing** — most AI sites position you as a prompt-writer. Martini positions you as a director. For pros, the fear isn't irrelevance — it's loss of creative control. Martini sells control back.
- **Creator name first, tool name invisible** — the showcase isn't "AI-generated videos," it's *"See what filmmakers are creating."*
- **Model partnerships as proof** — Kling, Veo 3, Sora framed as "filmmakers' favorite." Co-signs from the model layer below.

Worth asking before the rebuild: **which corner of this map is Zenvi?** The decision shapes nearly every concrete choice that follows — copy register, CTA strategy, hero visual, proof format, color palette.

## Takeaways for the Zenvi rebuild

Concrete moves to consider, ordered by impact:

1. **Pick one hero positioning sentence and let it do all the work.** Not a list of features. Identity, capability, or contrast — pick one mode. (Flora's "Your creative environment" is identity. Cardboard's "Edit videos at the speed of thought" is capability. Martini's "Your Film Set for AI Videos" is contrast.)
2. **Compress everything into three named acts.** Flora: Ideate/Iterate/Scale. Morphic: Canvas/Copilot/Compose. Whatever Zenvi does, three named nouns or verbs that someone could recite back to a friend after one visit.
3. **Hero visual = real product UI.** Not a gradient, not a 3D render. Whatever the actual interface looks like, screenshot it and put it directly under the headline.
4. **Workflow showcase, not feature list.** Three to six concrete *outcomes* a user could imagine themselves doing — quoted in product language, with the result visible.
5. **One free-low-commitment CTA + one demo/sales CTA.** Free CTA in the hero. Demo CTA only after value is established. Skip "Learn more."
6. **Trust through customer voice + recognizable logos.** Pull 2–3 specific outcome quotes (with numbers) from actual users. Then a logo strip. Skip generic 5-star testimonials.
7. **One philosophical line as the closer.** Flora's "A new medium needs a new canvas" is the move. Pick a sentence Zenvi can defend.
8. **Audience-aware nav if you have segments.** Mosaic's Founders/Creators/Marketers/Agencies sub-nav lets each visitor self-select into the right narrative. Only do this if Zenvi genuinely has distinct audiences.

## Open questions before designing

These are the decisions that change everything downstream. Worth answering before opening Figma:

- **Who's the primary visitor?** Single persona vs. multi-audience nav (Mosaic-style).
- **What's the one-line positioning?** Identity / capability / contrast — pick a mode.
- **What are the three named acts of the product?**
- **Free, gated, or demo-only?** Determines CTA strategy and exclusivity tone.
- **What's the trust format?** Customer-quote-led (Mosaic) vs. logo-gauntlet (Ali) vs. press-mentions (Flora) vs. named-creators (Martini).
- **Light or dark?** Five of six picked dark. Worth questioning the default.
- **Closing line?** The single sentence the visitor remembers a week later.
